1 Our commitment

CogniAI Studios, the company behind Jonah, will not let its products be used to harm children. We hold ourselves to these standards:

  • We prohibit child sexual abuse and exploitation (CSAE) and child sexual abuse material (CSAM) in every form, in every part of Jonah, for every user.
  • We act on every report we receive, and we put anything that suggests a child is at immediate risk at the front of the queue.
  • We report apparent CSAM and child endangerment to the relevant authorities, and we cooperate with their investigations.
  • We design Jonah so that it does not create a route for adults to contact, groom or exploit children.
  • We review these standards at least once a year, and after any serious incident, and publish the updated version at this address.

These standards apply to CogniAI Studios, its staff and contractors, and every feature of Jonah on Android and iOS.

2 What we prohibit

The following are banned in Jonah and on every service we run. Anyone under 18 counts as a child under these standards, whatever the local age of consent, and whatever a person says about their own age.

CSAMAny image, video, audio, text or other content that sexually depicts or describes a person under 18. This covers real, edited, drawn, animated and AI-generated material.

3 How Jonah protects young people

The strongest protection is a design that leaves abusers with nothing to work with. These are facts about how Jonah is built today.

No route for strangers

Jonah has no messaging, comments, friend lists, public profiles or content feeds. Nobody can contact, follow or find another user inside the app.

Nothing shared with others

You can attach a photo or file to a chat with the assistant, or send an image to Lens, but neither is ever shown to another user. Jonah does not host or publish what you submit.

No ads, no ad profiling

Jonah has no advertising SDK and does not collect an advertising ID. Nobody is shown targeted ads, and nobody’s behaviour is sold.

Trackers and pop-ups blocked

Built-in ad and tracker blocking stops many known ad networks and trackers from loading, and keeps pop-up ads from opening without a real tap.

Scam and phishing warnings

Trust Engine checks the reputation of the site you are on and warns you about suspected scams, the sort of sites criminals use to trick people, including young people.

Minimal permissions

Jonah does not ask for your location or contacts. The main sensitive permission is the microphone, for voice commands, plus the Google account picker on Android.

Straight talking

Jonah is a general-purpose web browser, not a filtered children’s browser. It does not host websites, and it does not block every adult site on the open internet. For younger users we recommend switching on device-level controls such as Google Family Link or Apple Screen Time alongside Jonah, and talking with them about what they see online.

4 The Noah assistant and Lens

Noah, Jonah’s AI assistant, and Lens, its media-source finder, send what you submit to our servers to produce an answer (the Privacy Policy explains exactly what). Because that content leaves the device, these standards apply to it directly.

  • You must not use Noah to create, request, or describe in a sexualised way any content involving a person under 18, or to get help finding it. That includes fictional, stylised and AI-generated depictions.
  • You must not send any image, video or text that sexualises a child to Lens or as a chat attachment.
  • Any attempt breaches these standards. We may restrict access from the device involved and, where the content is or appears to be CSAM, report it to the authorities.
  • If Noah ever produces something that sexualises a child, or suggests a child is being harmed, we want to hear about it at once. Use the details in section 5.

5 Reporting a concern

If you see or suspect child sexual abuse or exploitation, connected to Jonah or not, please act. Two routes matter, and you can use both.

If a child is in immediate danger, call the emergency services first. 999 in the UK · 911 in the US · or your local emergency number.

6 What we do when we find abuse

When a report reaches us, or we otherwise become aware of apparent CSAE, we follow the same steps every time.

  1. Triage

    Every report is logged and read by our Child Safety Lead. Anything suggesting a child is at immediate risk moves to the front of the queue.

  2. Contain

    We restrict access for the device or service involved and stop the content spreading. We do not open, copy or forward suspected CSAM beyond what the law requires.

  3. Report

    We report apparent CSAM and child endangerment to the relevant authorities: the National Crime Agency (UK), NCMEC’s CyberTipline (US), the Internet Watch Foundation and INHOPE hotlines, and local law enforcement where appropriate.

  4. Cooperate

    We keep only what the law requires us to preserve, and we respond promptly to lawful requests from law-enforcement and child-protection agencies.

  5. Learn and prevent

    We work out how it happened, close the gap, and update these standards and Jonah itself where needed.

If a report is about a website rather than Jonah itself, we also pass it to the Internet Watch Foundation or the relevant hotline, and we will block that address in Jonah as soon as we are able.

9 Who Jonah is for

Jonah is a general-audience app. It is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them (see section 7 of the Privacy Policy). Young people aged 13 to 17 should use Jonah with a parent or carer’s guidance where appropriate.

Whatever a user’s age, these standards protect everyone under 18.

10 Common questions

Can a stranger message my child through Jonah?

No. Jonah has no messaging, comments, friend lists or profiles, so there is nothing in the app for one user to use to reach another.

Does Jonah show ads or track my child?

No. Jonah has no advertising SDK and no analytics tracker of its own, and it blocks many known ad networks and trackers on the sites you visit.

Does Jonah filter adult websites?

Not fully. Jonah is a general-purpose browser and does not block every adult site on the open internet. Use Google Family Link or Apple Screen Time as well, and talk with your child about what they might come across.

Can Noah be used to make harmful content about children?

No. Using Noah to create, request or describe sexualised content involving anyone under 18 is prohibited. If Noah ever does produce something like that, please report it to us straight away.

What if I find abuse material on a site I opened in Jonah?

Close the page and do not share or screenshot it. Report the web address to the Internet Watch Foundation, and email it to us so we can act as well. If a child is in immediate danger, call the emergency services.

What happens after I report something?

We triage it, contain it, report it to the authorities where required, cooperate with any investigation, and fix whatever allowed it. Section 6 sets out each step.

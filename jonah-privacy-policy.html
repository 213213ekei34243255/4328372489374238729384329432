Privacy Policy

Effective 29 October 2026 Applies to Jonah on Android, iOS, Windows and Mac
In short

Jonah is a browser with an AI assistant built in. Your bookmarks and history stay on your device. When you use the assistant, the web agent, Trust Engine, or Lens, the specific thing you asked about — a message, a page's text, a domain, an image — is sent to our server to produce a response. We don't sell your data, we don't run ads in Jonah, and Jonah has no advertising or analytics tracker built in.

  • On your device: bookmarks, browsing history, saved settings, and the keys that protect your plan
  • Sent to our server: chat messages, page content you ask about, domains checked by Trust Engine, media submitted to Lens, and a device identifier used to apply free-trial limits
  • Handled entirely by the store you bought from (or Stripe / Razorpay on the direct-download desktop version): your payment details — we never see them
  • Protected throughout by encrypted connections, encrypted on-device storage, sandboxed web pages, and an assistant that stops and asks you before anything risky

3 Processed only on your device

A number of features do real work locally and send nothing anywhere unless you then choose to share the result in a chat message:

  • Text recognition and image labeling on photos you attach — this runs on-device (Apple's Vision framework on iOS, Google's ML Kit on Android, local text recognition on Windows and Mac), not a cloud service.
  • Reading PDF and text file attachments — extracted on your device before you decide whether to send it.
  • Scene detection in videos submitted to Lens — we pick out a handful of representative frames on your device rather than uploading the whole file.
  • Voice on Windows and Mac — speech-to-text and the assistant's spoken replies are both generated by models that run on your computer, so your voice never leaves it. The wake word, if you turn it on, is also detected locally, and the microphone is released the moment you turn it off.
  • Bookmarks, history and settings — kept on your device and removed when you clear them or uninstall.

4 How we use information

We use what you send us to do the thing you asked. Chat messages and page content go to our AI model to generate a reply. The page text the web agent sends lets it decide its next step. A domain you're visiting goes into Trust Engine's scoring so it can tell you whether a site looks trustworthy. An image goes into Lens's search so it can tell you where else that image appears online. The device identifier (and, on desktop, your Google account ID) is used to apply your plan and to enforce free-trial and daily limits — including making sure that uninstalling and reinstalling Jonah doesn't hand out a fresh free trial.

We don't build advertising profiles, we don't sell data to third parties, and Jonah has no ad SDK or analytics tracker built in.

5 Services we rely on

Some features work by calling other services on your behalf. Here's exactly which ones, and what each one sees:

  • Our own backend receives chat messages, web-agent steps, Trust Engine domain checks, Lens media, and the device identifier, and uses them to generate responses and apply plan limits.
  • A hosted AI model service — to write replies, our backend sends your message and the page content you asked about to a hosted large language model. It receives only what is needed to answer that request.
  • Google — web search results, search suggestions as you type, Lens image search, and, on Windows and Mac, Sign in with Google (identity only).
  • News and market-data providers — headlines come through our own news service; stock quotes are requested by ticker symbol only.
  • Radio stations — each station's own stream server delivers the audio and can see your IP address, as with any online radio.
  • Google Play Billing, the Apple App Store and the Microsoft Store — handle a subscription bought through that store entirely; we receive only a confirmation of which plan you're on.
  • Stripe and Razorpay — if you buy a plan in the direct-download desktop version, checkout happens on their secure payment pages; we never see your card number.
  • Update services — the stores update the store versions; the direct-download desktop version checks our official release channel for updates, which can see your IP address and app version.

6 How long we keep it

Bookmarks and history live on your device until you clear them yourself, in Settings. Chat, web-agent and Trust Engine requests are used to generate a response; each request carries a temporary session identifier that keeps a conversation coherent, together with the device identifier or account ID used to apply your plan's limits. We keep that identifier for as long as it is needed to enforce free-trial and usage limits — that is the entire point of it — and we don't use it for advertising.

Uninstalling Jonah removes everything stored on your device, including your saved settings and the on-device usage record. A reinstall does not reset your free-trial limits, because those are also tracked against your device identifier on our server. A purchased plan comes back through your store account (Restore Purchases) or, on Windows and Mac, by signing in with the same Google account.

7 Your choices and rights

  • Clear your browsing history any time from Settings → Data.
  • Turn ad and tracker blocking, the wake word, and the cursor animation on or off in Settings.
  • Deny the microphone, camera or (on Android) Google-account permission at the operating-system level — Jonah still works as a browser without them, just without voice commands or the account picker.
  • Stop the web agent or Computer agent at any moment with the on-screen STOP button (on desktop, the Escape key too). Touching or typing on the page while the assistant works pauses it so you can take over.
  • Uninstall the app to remove all on-device data. To also stop future backend processing, simply stop using the affected features before uninstalling.
  • Depending on where you live, you may have the right to ask what personal information we hold about you, to have it corrected or deleted, and to object to or restrict how it is used. To make a request, contact us using the address in section 11 and tell us which device or Google account it relates to.

9 Data security and cybersecurity

9.1 Encryption in transit

Every request from Jonah to our servers — chat messages, web-agent steps, Trust Engine checks, Lens media, plan checks — is sent over encrypted HTTPS (TLS) connections, so it can't be read or altered by someone on the same Wi-Fi network or by your internet provider. On Windows and Mac, Jonah refuses a website or server whose HTTPS certificate is invalid or untrusted rather than carrying on regardless, which is what protects you from someone impersonating a site on a shared or public network.

9.2 Encryption and protection of data stored on your device

Information that decides what you are allowed to use is never kept in plain text. On iOS it is held in the system Keychain, restricted to that one device. On Android it is held in encrypted storage backed by the Android Keystore. On Windows and Mac the private keys Jonah creates on your computer are encrypted using the operating system's own credential protection (Windows Data Protection / the macOS Keychain), so a copy of the file is useless on any other computer or user account. Your bookmarks, history and settings stay on your device and are removed when you clear them or uninstall.

9.3 Signed requests and server-side checks

Whether you may use a feature is never decided by the app alone — the app can be tampered with, so it isn't trusted to decide. The decision is made on our server. On Windows and Mac each plan check is signed with a key unique to your computer, so a copied or replayed request can't be used to pose as you, and each use is recorded with a one-time key so the same request can't be counted twice. If the check cannot be completed or verified for any reason, the answer is "no" — access fails closed instead of opening up. On mobile, purchases are verified by Google Play or the Apple App Store, and Jonah only recognises a plan the store has confirmed.

9.4 Secure sign-in

On Windows and Mac, signing in with Google opens Google's own page in your normal web browser, where you can check the real address — never an embedded window that could be faked. Jonah uses the industry-standard PKCE flow, so another program on your computer can't hijack the sign-in. We ask only for your identity (account ID, name, email) — never access to your mail, files, contacts or calendar — and Google's secret credentials are held on our server, not inside the app. Jonah never sees, handles or stores your Google password.

9.5 Safe browsing: isolation, blocking and warnings

  • Websites are kept away from the app. On Windows and Mac, every site you open runs in its own sandboxed, isolated view with no access to Jonah's internals or your files, developer tools switched off for web pages, and unsafe settings forced off whatever a page asks for. The app's own windows can only show files that ship with Jonah, so a website can never end up inside a window with Jonah's privileges.
  • Local helpers are locked down. The small helper services Jonah runs on your computer accept requests only from that same computer, serve only the few files they need, and refuse tricks such as disguised paths or requests from other websites.
  • Tracker, ad and pop-up blocking. Known tracking, analytics and advertising domains and unwanted pop-ups are blocked before they load, which stops those companies from following you from site to site. You can turn blocking off per your preference in Settings.
  • Trust Engine. Before you hand over information, Trust Engine checks a site against public sources and tells you if it looks like a scam or has a record of complaints, so you can leave before anything is lost.
  • Permissions on your terms. Microphone, camera and similar access is requested only when a feature or website needs it, and you can revoke it at any time in your operating system settings.

9.6 How the assistant protects you while it works

An assistant that can click and type for you must be harder to misuse than a person, not easier. These protections are enforced by Jonah's own code, not left to the AI model's judgement, so a model mistake — or a malicious web page — can't switch them off:

  • It asks before anything that matters. Purchases and payments, sending or posting something, deleting things, changing security or account settings, and (on desktop) uploading files all need your explicit approval first.
  • It never types your secrets. Noah will not type a password, a payment-card number or an API key. When a login or payment is needed it stops and hands over to you, and then continues after you tap Continue. What is in a password field is never read, shown in the activity list, remembered or sent anywhere. On Windows and Mac, anything you type back to the assistant that looks like a key or token is removed before it is sent or saved.
  • Web pages can't give it orders. Everything on a page is treated as untrusted. If a page contains hidden instructions aimed at the AI (a "prompt-injection" attack), they are flagged, and from then on anything that would send data elsewhere or leave the site needs your confirmation.
  • It stays on real web addresses. The assistant can only open ordinary web pages. It refuses file paths, scripts, internal addresses and app links, and re-checks the destination after every redirect.
  • You are always in control. A STOP button is on screen whenever it works (and Escape on desktop). If you touch the page, type, or switch tabs, it pauses so you can take over.
  • You can see what it did. The Activity view lists every step in plain words. On desktop, a local log of the actions it took is kept on your computer for you to review, with anything it typed redacted — it never records page content or screenshots.

9.7 Data minimisation by design

The safest data is data that was never sent. Photo and document text extraction, the video scene picker and (on Windows and Mac) speech recognition and speech output all run on your device. Bookmarks, history and settings stay on it. Requests to our server carry what that request needs and no more, and Jonah contains no advertising or analytics SDK that could collect anything else in the background.

9.8 App integrity and updates

The apps you install from Google Play, the Apple App Store and the Microsoft Store are signed, and updates come only through those stores, so you are not running a modified copy. On Android, Jonah can use Google Play's own update flow to require an update when a critical fix ships. On Windows and Mac the desktop app is locked so it can load only its own packaged code, can't be turned into a general scripting tool, and on macOS runs with Apple's Hardened Runtime. Downloaded desktop installers should only ever come from our official website or the stores.

9.9 If something goes wrong

If we learn of a security incident that affects your personal information, we will investigate promptly, fix the cause, and notify affected people and the relevant authorities where the law requires. If you find a security problem in Jonah, please email us at the address in section 11 with the details and give us a reasonable time to fix it before making it public — we take every report seriously.

9.10 What you can do

No system is perfectly secure, and Jonah is only one part of your security. You can help by keeping your phone or computer and Jonah up to date, using a screen lock, installing apps only from official stores or our website, and treating a "Waiting for you" prompt from Noah the way you would treat any request to sign in or pay: only continue if you started it.
